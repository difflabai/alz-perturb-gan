# Suggestion for the Prima Mente registration form

Draft answer for the optional field on the [Alzheimer’s Translation Challenge registration form](https://www.primamente.com/challenges/#register): “A gene or compound you’d like us to test in the lab, and why.” The challenge page describes combinatorial chemical and genetic perturbations of neurons, astrocytes, and microglia, with multimodal readouts. These notes favor perturbations for which that three-cell readout is still open.

Each candidate has two to four sentences of rationale and a label for how heavily it has already been studied. Links go to pages opened for this draft. DOIs and PMIDs are the identifiers on those pages or on the matching Europe PMC record.

## Candidates

### 1. INPP5D (SHIP1)

**Where it sits:** less studied than TREM2 or CD33, and the direction of a loss-of-function perturbation is still unsettled.

*INPP5D* encodes SHIP1, a microglia-enriched phosphatase that opposes TREM2/TYROBP signaling at PI(3,4,5)P3, and it is reported as a late-onset Alzheimer’s risk gene ([Iguchi et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10074152/); [Castranio et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10481344/); [Samuels et al. 2023](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10576836/); [Chou et al. 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-42819-w)). Reports of lowering it diverge by model and by timing: inducible knockdown after onset increased 6E10-positive deposit area and plaque-associated microglia in PSAPP mice, while microglial deletion begun before onset in 5xFAD increased plaque recruitment, containment, and amyloid-β engulfment, left amyloid burden unchanged, and reduced neuronal dystrophy ([Castranio et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10481344/); [Samuels et al. 2023](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10576836/)). Haploinsufficiency alone left amyloid deposition, plaque-associated microglia, astrocyte activation, and neurite dystrophy unchanged in NLGF mice, but on a *Tyrobp*-deficient background it restored microglial association with plaques, partially restored plaque compaction and astrogliosis, and reduced phosphorylated-tau-positive dystrophic neurites ([Iguchi et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10074152/)). In human iPSC-derived microglia, reduced INPP5D activity induced NLRP3 inflammasome assembly, caspase-1 cleavage, and secretion of IL-1β and IL-18, and loss of one copy changed synaptic gene expression in co-cultured neurons, so a neuron–astrocyte–microglia culture can measure the astrocyte response as well, including next to a TREM2-pathway perturbation ([Chou et al. 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-42819-w)).

### 2. Letrozole + irinotecan

**Where it sits:** underexplored as an Alzheimer’s combination. Letrozole and irinotecan are established cancer drugs; the paired hypothesis is from one 2025 paper.

Li et al. matched human single-nucleus Alzheimer’s signatures to Connectivity Map profiles from human cell lines and to electronic health records, then assigned letrozole, an aromatase inhibitor, to neurons and irinotecan, a topoisomerase inhibitor, to glia, including astrocytes, microglia, and oligodendrocyte precursor cells ([Li et al. 2025](https://www.cell.com/cell/fulltext/S0092-86742500737-8)). In 5xFAD mice crossed to a tau line (5xFAD/PS19), the combination improved memory and reduced Alzheimer’s-related pathology compared with vehicle and with either drug alone, and single-nucleus profiles showed cell-type-specific reversal of disease signatures, including *Apoe* in astrocytes and oligodendrocyte precursor cells. That functional test is the mouse model, the nominating signatures came from cell-line perturbations, and the authors discuss confounding in the cancer-patient records, so a human neuron–astrocyte–microglia culture is a direct check of the cell-type split this screen can run.

### 3. PLCG2 p.P522R

**Where it sits:** moderately studied. The protective allele points the same way in the papers below.

Sims et al. found a rare protective *PLCG2* variant, rs72824905 (p.Pro522Arg), in 85,133 people (odds ratio 0.68), in a gene they describe as highly expressed in microglia ([Sims et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5669039/)). Magno et al. localized PLCG2 mainly to microglia and dentate gyrus granule cells and measured a small hypermorphic effect of p.P522R in heterologous cells, which they read as a reason to activate PLCγ2 mildly ([Magno et al. 2019](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13195-019-0469-0)). Takalo et al. found that a mouse P522R knock-in raised PLC activity in myeloid cells, that BV2 cells overexpressing human PLCγ2-P522R phagocytosed more actively, and that the variant promoted immune functions associated with TREM2 signaling ([Takalo et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7488484/)). A knock-in versus loss comparison would be readable in tri-culture, and it ranks below *INPP5D* because these results already agree on mild gain of function.

### 4. TREM2

**Where it sits:** heavily studied.

Jonsson et al. and Guerreiro et al. independently reported that the rare *TREM2* variant rs75932628 (R47H) raises Alzheimer’s risk, with an odds ratio of 2.92 in the Icelandic discovery sample of Jonsson et al. ([Jonsson et al. 2013](https://www.nejm.org/doi/pdf/10.1056/NEJMoa1211103); [Guerreiro et al. 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23150934/)). Griciuc et al. later showed, in 5xFAD mice, that *TREM2* knockout worsened amyloid pathology and neurodegeneration and reduced clustering of Iba1-positive cells around plaques ([Griciuc et al. 2019](https://www.cell.com/neuron/fulltext/S0896-6273(19)30560-4)). TREM2 is the right reference arm beside *INPP5D*, because Iguchi et al. saw the haploinsufficiency effect on a TREM2-pathway-deficient background, and on its own it is the most heavily studied gene in this note.

### 5. CD33

**Where it sits:** heavily studied.

Griciuc et al. showed that *CD33* knockout reduced amyloid pathology and improved cognition in 5xFAD mice, that both effects were lost when *TREM2* was also knocked out, and that *CD33* knockout left the *TREM2*-knockout plaque-clustering defect in place ([Griciuc et al. 2019](https://www.cell.com/neuron/fulltext/S0896-6273(19)30560-4)). The same paper treats CD33 and TREM2 as microglial risk receptors, with TREM2 acting downstream of CD33. A CD33 arm in tri-culture would mostly retest that relationship, so it is a weaker new suggestion than *INPP5D*.

## Recommended pick

Test **INPP5D**, reduced in microglia, in neuron–astrocyte–microglia cultures.

The informative comparison is heterozygous reduction versus a stronger loss, with and without a TREM2-pathway perturbation. Chou et al. show that one missing copy changes neurons in microglia–neuron co-culture and that reduced activity releases IL-1β and IL-18. Iguchi et al. show that an *Inpp5d* reduction can look quiet until TREM2/TYROBP signaling is impaired. Astrocytes are the cell type those two experiments leave out, and they are part of this screen. Useful readouts are microglial state, IL-1β and IL-18, and the transcriptional response of neurons and astrocytes.

Letrozole plus irinotecan is the backup when the lab wants a chemical combination. It is the candidate that most directly matches a neuron-plus-glia drug pair, with the limits in that section.

### Paste-ready form answer

> Please test INPP5D (SHIP1) by reducing it in microglia inside neuron, astrocyte, and microglia cultures. Mouse studies report different outcomes of that reduction: inducible knockdown increased plaque deposits in PSAPP mice (PMID 36448627), microglial deletion in 5xFAD reduced neuronal dystrophy with amyloid burden unchanged (PMID 37061460), and haploinsufficiency left NLGF pathology unchanged unless TREM2/TYROBP signaling was already impaired, where it restored microglial plaque association and reduced pathological tau in dystrophic neurites (PMID 37035000). In human iPSC microglia, reduced INPP5D activity induces NLRP3 inflammasome activity with IL-1β and IL-18 release, and loss of one copy changes synaptic gene expression in co-cultured neurons (PMID 38016942), so a tri-culture can show whether those signals also reach astrocytes.

## Caveats

This is a learning-experiment suggestion for a registration form. It is not medical advice, and it is not a treatment recommendation. Nothing here is evidence that lowering INPP5D, or taking letrozole or irinotecan, would help a person with Alzheimer’s disease. Letrozole and irinotecan are cancer drugs.

A culture result describes this cell system. It does not establish clinical efficacy or safety. Mouse and iPSC experiments differ in timing, in how strongly the gene is reduced, and in which pathologies are present. Samuels et al. already note that their deletion began before disease onset, while the Castranio knockdown was induced later. The reason to run the experiment is to measure microglial state, inflammasome output, and the response of neurons and astrocytes in one human three-cell culture.
