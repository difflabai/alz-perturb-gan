01

What we built

A distribution of possible cells,
conditioned on pathology stage.

Microglia are immune cells in the brain. Their gene-expression patterns change with disease, but cells from the same person can also be very different. A model of their distribution can help us study both average changes and variation.

Our generator uses ParticleGAN 0.9.7, an adversarial learning method with a learned set of latent particles. In plain terms, a generator learns to produce plausible cell counts while a critic learns to distinguish generated and observed samples. All five fits started from scratch and completed 20,000 updates, with generator EMA disabled.

The input is one of four neuropathology stages: Not AD, Low, Intermediate, or High. The output is synthetic counts for a fixed panel of 2,000 variable genes. Training used 14,762 cells from 33 donors in the SEA-AD middle temporal gyrus dataset, covering six annotated microglia/perivascular-macrophage subtypes.

What a query means

“Generate a population resembling High-pathology cells” is a supported model query.

“Knock down CLEC5A” is not. The model has no gene-edit, compound, dose, genotype, or time input.

Its present use is to generate candidate associations, probe consistency, and prioritize follow-up experiments. A pathology-stage contrast does not predict a perturbation’s effect.

Exact model recipe

Released ParticleGAN 0.9.7 adversarial updates; 512 learned particles, an 8-dimensional latent, and 8-dimensional per-cell noise. A conditional count generator uses exact Gamma–Poisson sampling. Only pathology stage is populated in the seven-slot condition vector; the other six slots are zero. Generator learning rate 0.012; critic 0.018; particle 0.06; regularization coefficient 1; κ 1; prior regularization 0.01; EMA 0. The training critic operates on 150 bag features derived from 50 principal components, so a good projected score does not establish that every gene is accurate.

What these scores measure

The donor energy score asks whether a distribution of 26-dimensional generated donor summaries both covers observed summaries and has appropriate spread. The cell energy distance compares generated and real cell populations in a 50-dimensional PCA representation. Lower is better for both. Their numeric scales are different and should not be compared to each other.

Why a generator can still help

Resampling reuses observed cells. A generator gives a reusable conditional sampling model that can produce new count vectors and repeated synthetic populations. That is useful for analysis and prototyping. These results do not show that generated cells are more faithful than resampling, or that the model generalizes to unseen interventions.

The model reproduces some associations while generally attenuating their magnitude. The 2,000 genes are correlated measurements, not 2,000 independent replications.

Subtype composition also remains imperfect. Mean total-variation distances between generated and observed subtype mixtures are 0.194, 0.117, 0.067, and 0.128 across the four stages. Generated subtype labels come from a classifier with 80.6% apparent training accuracy, not verified cell identities or out-of-sample accuracy.

Selected hypothesis / CLEC5A

A consistent endpoint association.

CLEC5A is higher in High than Not AD in observed donors and in all five models. Its within-subtype effect remains positive after age, sex, APOE4 dosage, and postmortem-interval adjustment. The direction also agrees in an external human cohort.

But this is not a prediction that suppressing it will help. The intervention direction comes from published mouse experiments. Human microglial benefit remains the question to test.

What makes CLEC5A useful to discuss

It ranked 11th of 2,000 in the original within-subtype heuristic, and fifth among the 107 signals with primary external support. Its association stays positive in every donor-deletion check in both eligible subtypes. Those 42 checks reuse the same 21 endpoint donors; they are not 42 independent replications.

A 2024 study of CLEC5A deficiency reported improved amyloid clearance and pathological/behavioral outcomes in a mouse model. That gives an intervention rationale, while leaving an important human-cell translation gap. It is an existing biological lead, not a newly discovered target.

The cohort can fool a model.

Only three training donors are Not AD. Two are male; none carry APOE4. Of 18 High donors, four are male and 12 carry APOE4.

Several large signals involve sex-linked genes. USP9Y reverses direction after adjustment. CD163 does too. CH25H loses its sign in one control-donor deletion. We do not advance those signals on the strength of the generator alone.

05

A useful next experiment

Test function.
Don’t just move the expression marker.

For the Prima Mente Alzheimer’s Translation Challenge, the strongest proposal we can currently support is a focused human-cell test of CLEC5A reduction. The model supplies an association and its stability; published experiments supply the reason to test reduction.

01 / Intervention

Reduce CLEC5A in human microglia.

Use stem-cell-derived microglia, a non-targeting control, independent knockdown reagents, and target-engagement measurements. Include several donor backgrounds and APOE3/APOE4 where feasible. Use rescue or another orthogonal perturbation to probe specificity.

02 / Joint readouts

Separate uptake, degradation, and harm.

Measure amyloid uptake and its subsequent degradation separately, together with viability, inflammatory response, and synaptosome engulfment or synapse density in neuron co-culture. Add PLIN2/lipid-droplet and state-marker measurements as secondary readouts.

03 / A clear failure criterion

More uptake alone is insufficient.

A useful result would improve amyloid removal without reducing viability or increasing synapse loss. No functional benefit, increased inflammatory damage, or a result limited to one background would weaken the hypothesis. Set quantitative decision thresholds with the lab before running it.

Suggested registration answer

I’d like to test whether reducing CLEC5A in human stem-cell-derived microglia improves amyloid-β clearance without increasing synapse loss. Our analysis of human microglial gene expression, supported by a trained generative model, prioritizes CLEC5A as a pathology-associated candidate. Published mouse knockout results motivate testing whether the benefit translates to human cells, including across APOE3 and APOE4 backgrounds.

This proposal is exploratory and has not been submitted through this project. We are not affiliated with Prima Mente.

What would make the model more useful

The next model milestone is independent donor and regional replication, followed by perturbation data with molecular and functional readouts. That would let us test whether a model predicts a response to an intervention, rather than only generating stage-conditioned cells.

A fair test of added value

The shortlist also needs a matched analysis using real donor expression and published evidence without the generator. Our current filters explicitly use both. We have not shown that the generator discovers better targets than that simpler workflow.

The six non-stage condition slots in the training cache are zero placeholders. We recovered sex and APOE from original metadata for sensitivity checks; those attributes were not generator inputs.

The panel is incomplete.

Only two microglia/PVM subtypes meet the endpoint analysis requirement of at least three donors per stage and 20 cells per donor. Generated subtype assignments are classifier predictions.

Important genes including TREM2, APOE, INPP5D, ACSL1, ABCA1, and NLRP3 are absent from the 2,000-gene panel. The model cannot directly nominate or quantify them. Exact published marker-list coverage ranges from 0/30 antigen-presentation genes to 10/30 chemokine genes; broad pathway conclusions would overreach.

Inference and normalization

Five frozen 20,000-update models × eight Monte Carlo replicates × four stages × 200 bags × 400 cells produced 12.8 million unique synthetic cells. Common latent draws, library-depth templates, and Gamma/Poisson random seeds were shared across stages. Depth templates were drawn uniformly over training donors and were the same across stage queries. Gene expression is per-cell log1p(10,000 × gene count / total count across the measured panel), averaged within donor/subtype and then equally across donors and the two eligible subtypes. It is not whole-transcriptome absolute abundance.

A recovery preserved 87 completed cases and generated the remaining 73. Three whole-case replays, totaling an additional 240,000 physically generated cells, matched the retained arrays bit for bit. All 160 case hashes and 20 input hashes were checked; no model weights were updated. The unique inference total remains 12.8 million.

Exploratory ranking and donor sensitivity

The ranking heuristic is the smaller of the absolute observed and five-seed mean model effects, multiplied by model-seed direction agreement and real-donor deletion agreement. It is zero unless at least four of five models, 80% of 40 Monte Carlo contrasts, and 80% of the original groupwise donor deletions agree with the observed direction; the mean model direction must agree too. This is not a p-value, causal score, or drug score.

For the additional sensitivity filter, we fitted within each eligible subtype: donor mean expression ~ High indicator + standardized age + sex + APOE4 dosage + standardized postmortem interval, using the 21 High/Not AD donors. The adjusted direction must agree with the original contrast, and unadjusted and adjusted donor-deletion sign agreement must each be at least 80%, separately by subtype and deleted donor stage. With three controls, all three control deletions must agree. The six-parameter fits are full rank, but no APOE4 controls means adjustment relies on assumptions and extrapolation. No new gene-level p-values were calculated.

External cohort support is association, not intervention validation

The primary comparison uses published microglial general-pathology coefficients from the ROSMAP prefrontal-cortex study. Support means the same direction as the model/local association and published global FDR < 0.05. The author analysis adjusts for age, sex, postmortem interval, and latent factors. The study is a separate cohort/region from SEA-AD, but we have not performed a subject-identity crosswalk. Published amyloid and tangle associations are secondary checks from the same cohort, not additional independent replications.

We used the author repository at commit 28388f829be6affa1578e56fa09c4be8cd273817. The complete preprocessing and differential-expression pipeline has not been reproduced here. The ROSMAP microglial association can still include within-cell-class composition effects.
