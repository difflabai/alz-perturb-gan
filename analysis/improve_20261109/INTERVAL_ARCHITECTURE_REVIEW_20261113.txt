Interval construction and network architecture review, 2026-11-13
================================================================

Request: explain nominal intervals, inspect what produces them, and identify
changes that could improve a donor-distribution model and patient feature vectors.
This is a frozen audit: no optimizer updates, no default changes, no sealed donors.

What the intervals actually mean
--------------------------------
For each target donor, the evaluator draws 32 synthetic donors at matched
cell depth. Each synthetic donor contributes 100 summary coordinates: the
mean and SD of each of 50 train-fitted PCs across 64 generated cells. For
each coordinate, the 10th and 90th sample percentiles define a central range.
Coverage is averaged over donor-coordinate pairs and 24 real-cell subsamples.
It is not an 80% probability statement about the whole patient, a clinical
outcome, all genes simultaneously, or uncertainty in the model's weights.

The earlier combined 66-68% coverage description was too coarse. Across all
18 current-default fits (9 overlapping folds, 2 seeds):
  Expression-pattern means:   82.46% covered
  Cellular spread (PC SDs):   51.14% covered
  Combined:                  66.80% covered
For SD coordinates, 45.77% of observations lie BELOW the predicted lower
bound and only 3.09% above the upper bound. This is predominantly a level/
generalization error in cellular heterogeneity, not simply narrow uncertainty
about donor means.

Finite-sample construction also contributes. For iid perfect Uniform(0,1)
samples, the exact expected coverage of NumPy's interpolated 10/90 sample
percentiles is .8*(M-1)/(M+1): .7515 at M=32, .7876 at M=128. A 50,000-trial
simulation reproduces this. This example is not an exact correction for every
distribution or for the fixed snapshot/stage mixture used by the GAN.
Using order-statistic ranks 3 and 30 at M=32 has iid perfect-model marginal
coverage 27/33=.8182. Applied diagnostically, it raises current combined
coverage to 73.32%, with intervals about 16.7% wider; mean coverage becomes
87.49% and SD coverage 59.15%. It does not repair the cellular-spread model.
Historical scoring was not replaced.

What the current architecture does
----------------------------------
Generator: an 8-dimensional donor particle plus a seven-slot condition vector
(only pathology stage active), through a width-128 donor MLP into FiLM scale/
shift. Independent 8-dimensional cell noise enters a width-128 cell layer.
A shared nonlinear head emits 2,000 softmax gene proportions, scaled by library
size. Each gene has ONE shared NB dispersion parameter. Gamma-Poisson sampling
produces counts. There is no patient encoder, explicit microglial cell-state
mixture, or separately identified biological patient-feature vector.

Critic: 150 bag features from 50 train-selected PCs (means, SDs and Fourier
means), plus the 2,000-gene pseudobulk block, into a width-128 MLP. The selected
read-out mixes late training snapshots and half own-stage/half train-stage
conditions; bias calibration changes output gene levels only.

The new audit does not justify repeating a wider/deeper-network screen.
Earlier matched capacity probes already found no clear gain from width,
depth or a larger cell latent. Donor-dependent dispersion was also tried;
its extra-fold primary gain did not confirm. It is not an untested fix.
The current snapshot mixture's projected mean covariance has similar
participation ratio to real summaries in this audit, but that includes cell
sampling noise and multiple model states. It is not evidence that its latent
variables are identifiable or free of single-state collapse.

A feature-space issue must be resolved before architectural claims
------------------------------------------------------------------
Real train donors have mean PC SD .8983; held-out donors .7386. Generated
donors have .9019 on train targets and .9007 on held-out targets: they mostly
reproduce the training feature-space variance.

As a real-data-only control, project the SAME genes and SAME train-fitted
gene standardization through 50 fixed random orthogonal directions. Choose
the directions independently of expression, normalize only on train cells,
and use the same donor/64-cell subsamples. The real held-out/train SD ratio:
  Train-selected PCA directions: .8223
  Fixed random directions:       1.0006

This implicates feature selection or donor-specific covariance in the
PCA representation. It does not prove PCA alone caused the generator error;
the two direction sets measure different structures. It does show that a
model can match training PCA variability and still miss new donors, while
the same raw cells show no comparable aggregate shift in the control.
Simply increasing noise or widening every interval is poorly targeted.

Frozen decoder interventions
-----------------------------
Six fits on original folds 0-2, seeds 0-1. Use the same donor latents, stage
conditions, library draws and cell-noise inputs. Reuse each fit's existing
training-only output-bias calibration. Change only the indicated count-head
quantity; do not retrain or refit calibration. A new common count stream is
used for these local probes, so their baseline is not byte-identical to the
historical generation. Summary measurements:

                              PC-SD coverage  generated mean SD  real mean SD
  Baseline                       .5562             .8854            .7433
  Half network mean variation    .6459             .7497            .7433
  Remove network mean variation  .6174             .6902            .7433
  NB shape multiplied by 4       .3824             .9926            .7433
  Poisson limit                  .2984            1.0531            .7433

Mean contraction preserves each 256-cell bag's expected gene totals and each
cell's expected library, using the same formula as the earlier count-shape
experiment. Reducing Gamma-Poisson overdispersion does NOT monotonically
reduce SD after log normalization and PCA; it worsens this local metric.
These are diagnostic interventions, not validated new default recipes.

What to test next, in order
---------------------------
1. Keep the historical raw score, and report separate mean/SD coverage,
   widths and proper interval score. Add a larger-M or order-statistic
   comparison for interval-estimation bias. For calibrated coverage claims,
   use calibration DONORS excluded from model training, plus separate test
   donors. Training-fitted interval shifts/width factors reached only .7287
   held-out combined coverage here and have no conformal guarantee.
   Donor dependence must remain intact; treating cells as independent patients
   cannot supply the missing calibration sample size. Conformal guarantees
   require their stated exchangeability assumptions.

2. First fresh-training ablation: change the critic's observation space,
   preserving generator width, recipe, feature count, block scale and update
   rule. Compare 50 train-selected PCs with 50 fixed random orthogonal
   projections; keep the same mean/SD/Fourier layout and pbulk block. Keep the
   evaluator's PCA space separate from the training observation space.
   A cross-donor-validated gene-program basis is another subsequent arm.
   Score coverage and sharpness separately for donor means and cellular
   spread, plus gene-level means, detection, variance and covariance. A
   higher coverage number obtained only by wider intervals is not sufficient.

3. If the observation ablation leaves a decoder limitation, test a modest
   hierarchical decoder that separates donor gene-program activity,
   donor-specific cell-state mixture, within-state cell variation and count
   dispersion. The current FiLM path entangles these effects. Avoid merely
   enlarging the MLP or re-enabling the already unconfirmed dispersion arm.
   Test one added component at a time with fresh matched fits and protected
   donor splits before promotion.

4. For patient feature vectors, add a permutation-invariant DONOR encoder
   over a set of cells, or explicitly validate an inverse-fitting procedure.
   The current generator supplies latent samples but cannot directly encode
   a real patient's cells. Validate split-cell repeatability, reconstruction
   of held-out genes/cells, depth sensitivity and reproducibility across
   seeds. Separate nuisance/technical variation from proposed biological
   programs; an interpretable name on a latent axis is not validation.

No new training screen was launched and no default was changed in this audit.
The next architecture experiment is defined above; its performance is unknown.
